Context and assumptions

  • Functional length (Figure 46-4H): rural arterial LT + LD + LS; urban arterial, other facility, and stop or T facility LT + LD + LS desirable, LT + LS minimum. LD is a consideration only at a free-flowing leg of a stop-controlled or signalized intersection, or a free-flowing turning roadway with a turn lane.
  • Taper (46-4.02(02) item 1): 100-ft straight-line taper for a single turn lane; 150 ft for dual turn lanes on an urban street. On a curvilinear alignment design the taper with a constant rate of divergence, at least 50 ft.
  • Deceleration (item 2, Figure 46-4J, Rev. Aug. 2018): required on rural facilities, desirable on urban facilities. Multiply LD by the grade-adjustment factor (downgrade 1.00 to 1.35; upgrade 1.00 to 0.80). Under restricted urban conditions deceleration may occur in the travel lane (LD = 0). Figure 46-4J covers 25 to 60 mph.
  • Signalized storage (item 3): 2.0 times the average vehicles arriving per cycle for cycles under 120 s, 1.5 times for 120 s or longer, at 20 ft per vehicle; at least two passenger cars. Alternatively use the Figure 46-4K(1) chart (C = 75 s, v/c = 0.80) times the Figure 46-4K adjustment factor. Turn-on-red or a separate right-turn phase may allow a shorter right-turn lane.
  • Unsignalized storage (item 4, Figure 46-4L): enough for the turning vehicles arriving in an average 2-minute period in the design hour; at least two passenger cars, or one car and one truck if trucks exceed 10%.
  • Minimum length (item 5): under restricted conditions, the full-width lane (deceleration plus storage) may be 50 ft with fewer than 10% trucks, or 100 ft with 10% or more, excluding the taper. At signals the turn lane should exceed the queue in the adjacent through lane.
  • The storage-length chart in the IDM is a graph and is not digitized here; enter a value read from it if you use that method. In the PDF the adjustment-factor table carries the caption "Figure 46-4K(1)".
