Taper Lengths

Computes merging (lane-drop) and lane-shift taper lengths from the IDM longitudinal taper rates (Figure 502-2J), the lane-drop taper and acceleration distance for an added through lane at an intersection (Figure 46-7A), and lists the fixed taper lengths the IDM uses for turn lanes, TWLTL transitions and downstream recovery.

Inputs

Merging or shifting taper (Figure 502-2J)
Added through lane at an intersection (Figure 46-7A)

Context and assumptions

Figure 502-2J, Longitudinal Taper Rate and Length (Rev. Aug. 2018)

L = merging taper rate × offset distance
  • Merging taper rates: 10:1 (20–25 mph), 15:1 (30), 20:1 (35), 30:1 (40), 45:1 (45), and V:1 from 50 to 70 mph.
  • Merging taper (lane drop) and lane-shift taper: L as above. Two-way left-turn lane transition taper: 100 ft. Downstream recovery taper: 50 ft per lane (desirable 100 ft per lane).
  • Section 502-2.02(10): Figure 502-2J gives the minimum taper rate and length for lane reductions; the edge-line taper rate applies across a bridge.

Figure 46-7A, Extension of Additional Through Lanes

For an added through lane carried through a signalized intersection, DE is the distance to accelerate from a stop to 6 mph below the average running speed (increase where driveways are present); the lane-drop taper follows. Sharper tapers may be used in urban areas but should be at least 300 ft. These are preliminary-design criteria; final design is case by case.

Turn lanes and acceleration lanes (Chapter 46)

  • Turn-lane entrance taper: 100-ft straight-line taper for a single turn lane, 150 ft for dual turn lanes on an urban street; on a curve, constant divergence, at least 50 ft (46-4.02(02)).
  • End of a parallel acceleration lane: 300-ft taper (46-6.03). Turn-lane extension on a 2-lane highway: 300-ft tapered acceleration lane, not less than 200 ft under restricted conditions (46-4.02(05)).
