Context and assumptions

Stopping sight distance (IDM 42-1.0)

SSD is the distance traveled during the brake reaction time plus the distance needed to brake to a stop:

SSD = 1.47 V t + 1.075 V² / a    (Equation 42-1.1)
  • t = 2.5 s (covers 90% of drivers in simple to moderately complex conditions); a = 11.2 ft/s².
  • The IDM prints the first term as .147Vt; the values in Figure 42-1A use 1.47Vt, which is what this page uses.
  • Grade adjustment is not required by INDOT (42-1.01 item 3).
  • Height of eye 3.5 ft, height of object 2 ft (passenger car taillights).
  • The minimum SSD for a passenger car is a Level One design-exception criterion (Section 40-8.02). Longer SSD should be used where practical; trucks on long downgrades with horizontal sight restrictions warrant more than Figure 42-1A (42-1.03).

Decision sight distance (IDM 42-2.0)

Consider DSD at complex locations where reaction time may exceed 2.5 s: exit or entrance gores, lane drops, left-side freeway entrances or exits, railroad grade crossings, approaches to detours or lane closures, toll plazas, and intersections that need unusual maneuvers. Columns A and B use Equation 42-1.1; columns C, D and E use DSD = 1.47 V t (Equation 42-2.1). Figure 42-2A starts at 30 mph.

Passing sight distance (IDM 42-3.0)

Applies to 2-lane, 2-way highways only, measured from a 3.5-ft eye to a 3.5-ft object. It is not the MUTCD no-passing-zone distance. Figure 42-3C gives recommended percent of rural project length with passing sight distance.
