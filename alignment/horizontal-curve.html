Context and assumptions

Minimum radius (IDM 305-3.03)

R = V² / [15 (e + f)]    (Equation 305-3.1)

The minimum radius uses emax and the limiting side-friction factor fmax listed at the top of Figures 305-4B through 305-4E. The IDM tabulated minimum radius (last row of each table) governs; the equation value is shown as a check. Using the minimum radius should be avoided where practical because it forces the maximum superelevation rate. For rural and high-speed facilities, radii in the dark-shaded area of the 6% and 8% tables should be used only where unusual design circumstances dictate.

Selecting emax (Figure 305-4A)

  • Rural, all speeds: 6% (8% may be retained on reconstruction unless the curve has an unacceptable superelevation-related crash history).
  • Rural frontage roads: 8%. High-speed urban (V ≥ 50 mph): 6%.
  • Low-speed urban (V ≤ 45 mph, except ramps), turning roadways, approaches to roundabouts/DDIs: Method 2, Figure 305-4E.
  • System ramps 6% (loops may use 8%); service ramps 6% or 8%; last exit-ramp curve to a stop/signal terminal 4% or 6%.
  • Chapter 53 note: superelevation should not exceed emax = 8% because of snow and ice.

Length of curve (IDM 305-3.02)

  • High-speed limited-access facilities: 30V desirable, 15V minimum.
  • Other high-speed facilities: 15V minimum.
  • Low-speed facilities: 15V where practical; shorter curves are expected at roundabouts and Method 2 urban facilities.

Curve elements (Figure 305-6B)

T = R tan(Δ/2)   L = πRΔ/180   E = R/cos(Δ/2) − R   LC = 2R sin(Δ/2)   M = R[1 − cos(Δ/2)]   D = 5729.58/R

PC = PI − T, PT = PC + L. Other limits in Chapter 305: compound-curve radius ratios (305-3.04(02)), broken-back tangent 1500 ft on freeways or 15V elsewhere (305-3.04(03)), and 300 ft minimum between reverse curves with a continuously rotating plane (305-3.04(04)).

The default inputs reproduce IDM Figure 305-6C (Δ = 38.34°, D = 3°30', PI = 20+25.36) when R is set to 1637.02 ft.
