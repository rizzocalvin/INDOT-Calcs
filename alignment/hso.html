Horizontal Sight-Line Offset (HSO)

Computes the clear distance needed on the inside of a horizontal curve so that the design sight distance is available (Equation 305-5.1), and checks an existing obstruction offset.

Diagram

Plan view. The sight line is a chord of length S along the inside-lane path; HSO is its middle ordinate. Entering and exiting clearance connects to the outside edge of shoulder S/2 before the PC and beyond the PT (305-5.03(01)); shown schematically.

Context and assumptions

HSO = R [1 − cos(28.65 S / R)]    (Equation 305-5.1, angle in degrees)
  • HSO is measured from the center of the inside travel lane to the obstruction; R is the radius to the center of the inside travel lane; S is the design sight distance (305-5.03).
  • Passenger car: eye 3.5 ft, object 2 ft, both at the center of the inside lane. For ramps with lanes wider than 12 ft, place eye and object 6 ft from the inside lane edge (305-5.02).
  • The equation applies where L > S, from PC to PT. Where L ≤ S, check sight distance graphically, including the area from S/2 before the PC to S/2 beyond the PT (305-5.03 item 2 and 305-5.03(01)).
  • Sight obstructions include guardrail, bridge railing, median barrier, walls, cut slopes, wooded areas, buildings, tall crops, and the right-of-way line. Point obstacles such as a sign or utility pole are not sight obstructions (305-5.01).
  • Shoulders should not be widened beyond 12 ft solely to meet HSO (305-5.01).
  • The calculation is plan view only. A three-dimensional evaluation is recommended for compound curves, overlapping horizontal and vertical curves, and barriers within the HSO (305-5.03(02)). NCHRP Report 910 is recommended for assessing restrictions (305-5.04).
